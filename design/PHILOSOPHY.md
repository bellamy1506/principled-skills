# What a skill is underneath

A working philosophy for making skills. It was distilled from
building two of them, `panel-review` and `council-testing`, out of two
months of real use, and from getting the first one wrong before getting it
right. It is written for whoever makes the next skill, person or agent, for
a purpose that may look nothing like those two.

Nothing here is a rule. Each section is a question worth asking, with what
the question has been worth so far. Take what fits.

---

## 1. A skill carries a purpose. The procedure is only its current body.

Every skill has steps. The steps are not the skill. They are one way of
serving a purpose, chosen under one set of tools, costs and habits, and all
of those change.

The first draft of the panel skill was assembled faithfully from the
written rules of the project it came from. It was wrong at the centre. The
rules said "consensus"; the purpose had always been something else, which
the rules had never needed to say because the person who wrote them already
knew it. A procedure can be copied perfectly while the reason for it is
lost.

So find the purpose first, and find it in the right places: in what the
owner said when they first asked for the thing, in what they corrected, and
in what actually happened when it was used. Rule documents, templates and
tooling are evidence about one implementation. Treat them as that.

*Ask: if every step in this skill were replaced, what would have to stay
true for it to still be this skill?*

## 2. Name where the value comes from, and guard only that.

A skill pays for itself through one or two mechanisms. Everything else in
it is support.

The panel's value comes from **difference**: three minds made to think
unlike each other. The council's comes from **attempt and measurement**:
finding out by doing, with a number as judge. Once the source is named,
most design questions answer themselves. Does this change make the three
reports more alike? Then it is wrong, however sensible it looks. Does this
let something other than a measurement count as a result? Wrong.

A skill with no named source of value collects rules, because nothing says
which ones matter.

*Ask: what, exactly, does this skill produce that would not exist without
it? What would silently destroy that?*

## 3. Know the instrument by its edges.

A skill is understood best by what it is not. The panel became clear when
set against the council: one thinks differently, the other does
differently. Both became clearer against the cheaper things beside them: a
single agent, a single command, evidence already on disk.

An instrument that does not say when to use something smaller will be used
for everything. The most common waste found in the records was a heavy
instrument applied to a job that one agent, or one command, would have
done.

*Ask: what is the cheapest thing that could answer this instead? What is
the neighbouring skill, and where is the line between them?*

## 4. Whoever judges is the weakest point.

An author cannot see its own work. A builder tests what it meant to build,
so its tests pass. A reviewer handed the author's theory finds evidence for
the theory. An orchestrator asked to trim the unreasonable trims whatever
lies outside its own habits, which is exactly what it was supposed to be
looking for.

Every skill that produces a judgement needs an anchor outside the one
judging: a measurement nobody can edit, an attacker who built none of it, a
mind deliberately set at a different angle, or the owner. Where no anchor
can be had, the skill should make the judge show its work: what it left
out, why, and what would have to be true for the rejected thing to be
right.

*Ask: who decides this is good? What do they have to check against,
besides their own sense that it is?*

## 5. Sameness is the default. Difference has to be made.

Capable models share their training, so they share their answers. Ask
several of them and you mostly get one answer several times. This is fine
when the common answer is what you want. It is a wall when the thing you
need lies outside it.

If a skill uses more than one agent, it should know why more than one, and
what makes each of them not a copy of the others. A forced premise; a
different road to the same mark; a separate patch of ground. Without that,
the extra agents are repetition at full price. With it, they can reach
things no single run reaches, and their overlap becomes a signal worth
reading: agreement from genuinely different starting points means
something, and agreement from the same starting point means nothing.

*Ask: if two of these agents returned the same thing, would that be
success or waste?*

## 6. Reach for what the ordinary loop cannot.

Most work improves by "the same, a little better". That loop is valuable
and it has a ceiling, because it never leaves the shape it started in.

Some skills exist to leave the shape. A mark set past what seems possible
keeps a capable worker searching after the obvious is spent. A lens that
looks wrong for the problem can come back holding something the right
lenses would never pick up. Neither is likely to pay on any one run. The
skill is built so that it can.

This changes how failure reads. A goal not reached, an idea not used, a
round that found nothing: each is information, if the skill makes it say
what blocked it or what would have made it work. A skill that only knows
how to report success will learn to manufacture it.

*Ask: is this skill for doing the known thing well, or for finding a
better thing? If the second, where does it make room for the unlikely?*

## 7. What each participant believes is part of the design.

An agent acts on what it has been told. Tell a reviewer its angle is the
doubtful one and it stops pushing. Tell a builder the target was set out of
reach on purpose and it stops at the first decent gain. Hand a reviewer
your hypothesis and you get your hypothesis back three times.

So decide, for every participant, what it needs to know, what it needs to
believe, and what it must not be handed. Then look for the leaks, because
they are rarely in the obvious place. Information reaches an agent through
its brief, and also through a skill's public description, through files it
can open, through the name on a marker line, through a sibling's report.

There is a line here and it matters. This is about framing and
withholding, never about false statements of fact. A builder is told the
goal is hard and can be reached; that is a stance the work has earned many
times, and no fact is misstated. The owner is never managed this way.
Everything is said plainly to the person the work is for.

*Ask: for each agent in this skill, what would change its behaviour if it
knew? Which of those would be a loss? How could it find out?*

## 8. Tight in some places, loose in others, and know which is which.

A skill that is strict everywhere stifles the thing it was built to
produce. A skill that is loose everywhere gets gamed, or drifts. The work
is in telling the two kinds of place apart.

**Tighten what protects truth, independence and authority:** the measure
and who may touch it; what counts as evidence; what one mind is allowed to
pass to another; who may start, spend, merge or decide between two valid
designs.

**Loosen where the thinking happens:** how an agent goes about its part;
where it looks; whether it may leave the expected method for a better one;
how much it may speculate, once speculation is labelled as such.

Constraint and freedom are not opposites here. A firm boundary with open
ground inside it produces more invention than open ground with no
boundary; an artist is not less creative for being held to a medium. Two
failures, from the records, in opposite directions: a goal with no guard
clause, won by cheating the number; and a brief so fenced that the reviewer
could not follow its own premise to where the answer was.

*Ask of every rule in the skill: does this protect honesty, or does it
fence thought? Is it in the right list?*

## 9. A check where a check can exist. A reason where it cannot.

A rule that is only a sentence gets broken, usually within the session
that wrote it. A rule that is a check does not. So wherever a behaviour
can be enforced mechanically, by a script that refuses, a tool that an
agent lacks, a file it cannot change, build that, and keep the sentence
short.

But much of what matters in a skill cannot be checked, because it depends
on a situation the skill's author never saw. Projects differ in what is
slow, what is costly, what is dangerous. There the skill should give its
reason instead of a number, so that whoever applies it can make the call
the author would have made. A limit expressed as "whatever would catch a
loop without stopping honest work, in the unit that fits this project" is
more useful than any fixed figure.

Checks hold the line. Reasons carry judgement across the gap. A skill
needs both, and a rule with neither an incident behind it nor a check in
front of it should be left out.

*Ask: can this be made impossible to violate? If not, have I said why it
matters well enough that someone could apply it to a case I did not
imagine?*

## 10. Combination is where the surplus is.

When several results come back, the easy things to do are pick one or
average them. Both throw away most of what was bought.

The gain is in putting parts together: the strong element of one report
with the practical detail of another; a by-product that failed at its own
purpose and solves someone else's problem; working changes from three
roads stacked on one copy. Whoever combines needs the general judgement the
contributors gave up or did not need, and needs to be suspicious of their
own taste while doing it.

And a combination is a new thing. Gains do not simply add, and parts that
each worked alone can collide. Whatever was combined gets checked again as
a whole.

*Ask: after the parts come back, who puts them together, with what
judgement, and how is the whole then tested?*

## 11. Cost is a decision, and it belongs to someone.

Expensive instruments are worth having. They are not worth having by
accident. The owner decides when to spend; the skill may propose, with the
price and the reason, and then waits. Having been asked, it does not then
spend more inside the work to prevent things that do not matter: a real
break gets fixed, a harmless once-in-a-decade oddity gets written down.

Match the price to where the value comes from. Where value comes from
structure, from difference between minds, cheap models do the work and
strong ones add little. Where value comes from capability, from building
something that has to hold, pay for capability. Keep every repeated step
fast, because a slow step repeated by every agent is the largest cost
there is.

A limit should catch a loop, not stop a struggle. Hard problems sometimes
need the same wall hit many times from different sides. They never need
the same run a third time.

*Ask: who chose to spend this? What is the money buying, structure or
capability? What would a runaway look like, and what stops it?*

## 12. Understand before building, and prove the understanding.

The cheapest moment to be wrong is before anything is written. The first
skill here was drafted on a confident misreading and had to be turned
around. The second was not drafted at all until its purpose had been
stated back in plain words, with analogies, and corrected.

Stating a purpose in your own words, without the owner's phrases to lean
on, shows whether you have it or are only carrying it. An analogy shows it
better still, because an analogy can only be built from the structure of an
idea, not its wording. Open choices go to the owner in one list, each with
a recommendation, so that a decision is never made by default.

When the owner corrects you, the correction is usually larger than it
looks. It tends to reveal that a whole layer was read wrong, and it is
worth going back through everything built on that layer.

*Ask: can I explain what this is for to someone who has never seen it,
using none of the words I was given? What have I assumed that nobody
confirmed?*

## 13. Leave the next maker what they need, where they will find it.

A skill is read by different readers who need different things, and
putting it all in one place serves none of them.

- **The public description** is seen by everything, including agents the
  skill will later instruct. It says when to use the skill and nothing
  about how it works on its participants.
- **The skill body** is for the agent running it: the procedure, and the
  reason for each step, so judgement can fill the gaps.
- **References** are loaded only when needed: templates, variants, the
  incident behind each rule.
- **A philosophy file** is for whoever changes the skill later. It holds
  the purpose, the ways the skill has gone wrong, and a short list of
  tests any change must pass. It is the thing that stops a later edit,
  made in a fresh context with good intentions, from sanding the purpose
  off.
- **A readme** is for people.
- **Scripts** hold whatever is mechanical, so that no agent spends
  judgement on it.

Words are part of this. A term that was natural in one project can
mislead in the next; a "weighting" suggested a dial where a forced premise
was meant, and "consensus" nearly rebuilt a skill around the wrong goal.
Choose names for what the thing is, and change them when they stop
fitting.

*Ask: who will read each part, and what do they need from it? If someone
edits this in a year with none of today's context, what would keep them on
course?*

## Underneath all of it

Three things recur in every section above.

**The purpose is not in the artefacts.** It is in a person, and has to be
drawn out, stated back, and written down where the next maker will look.

**Minds, including artificial ones, do what their situation makes
natural.** A skill is mostly the design of situations: what each
participant sees, believes, may touch, and is judged by.

**Honesty needs structure, and invention needs room.** Good skills are
strict about the first so that they can be generous with the second.

A skill built for something entirely unlike a panel or a council, a way of
writing, of diagnosing, of teaching, of operating a machine, will share
none of their steps. It can still ask what it is for underneath its steps,
where its value comes from, who judges and against what, what each
participant should believe, where to be strict and where to be generous,
and what to leave for whoever comes next.
